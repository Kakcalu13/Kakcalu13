## A short story

I started, like a lot of us, with a Raspberry Pi and the patience to keep wiring things until they worked. From there: Arduino, ROS, Gazebo, robotic arms, drones, industrial automation. Then macOS — first Intel, then Apple Silicon. Then a stranded simulator I refused to give up on, and twelve repositories' worth of stubborn, satisfying work to bring it back.

Today the work I'm proudest of is also the smallest in lines of code: a speech-to-text model for the language of my wife and our children — because none existed before.

I work in public. I love collaborating. I read every issue and reply to every email.

## 🪶 The mission: Mizo language technology

Mizo is the language of my wife and our children — a Tibeto-Burman language of Northeast India. I'm still a learner. I can write Mizo, but **I cannot yet ask my computer to listen to it: there is no speech-to-text for Mizo.** No Siri, no Translate parity, almost no public datasets. The phrase *"there isn't one yet"* applies to most of the technology I take for granted in English. So I'm building it, slowly, stubbornly, joyfully:

- **[mizo_ai_speech_to_text](https://github.com/Kakcalu13/mizo_ai_speech_to_text)** — a speech-to-text pipeline for Mizo, trained on hand-labelled recordings.
- **[tawng](https://github.com/Kakcalu13)** *(in active development)* — a mobile speak-and-listen app.
- **[Mizo-godot-game](https://github.com/Kakcalu13/Mizo-godot-game)** — a Godot game teaching Mizo, English, and sign to my children.
- **[Inkhelhna-ho-te](https://github.com/Kakcalu13/Inkhelhna-ho-te)** — *"let's play"* — a simple game in Mizo, for the joy of it.

> **If you work on low-resource NLP, speech models, or language preservation,** please reach out. The work is too important to do alone, and there is so much room for collaboration here.
