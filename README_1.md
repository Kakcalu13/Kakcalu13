## What I believe

**A language is software, too.** It needs maintainers, datasets, training, and tools. When no one is shipping for it, that's the work — and that's why most of what I do begins, in some small way, with **Mizo**.

**Stranded technology is not dead technology.** Apple Silicon left a generation of robotics simulators behind. I disagreed. Twelve repositories later, Ignition Citadel runs natively on M-series Macs.

**The best way to teach children is to put their language in their hands.** So I'm building games my kids can actually play — in Mizo, in English, in sign.

**Open source is a community, not a portfolio.** I read every issue. I welcome every PR. The door is open.

---

## 🪶 The work I care about most: Mizo language technology

Mizo is the language of my wife and our children — a Tibeto-Burman language of Northeast India. I'm still a learner. I can write Mizo, but **I cannot yet ask my computer to listen to it: there is no speech-to-text for Mizo.** No Siri, no Translate parity, almost no public datasets, almost no tooling. Most of the technology I take for granted in English begins, in Mizo, with the words *"there isn't one yet."*

So I'm building — slowly, stubbornly, joyfully — the tools my family and community will need:

- **[mizo_ai_speech_to_text](https://github.com/Kakcalu13/mizo_ai_speech_to_text)** — a speech-to-text pipeline for Mizo, trained on recordings I gather and label by hand.
- **tawng** *(in active development)* — a mobile speak-and-listen app for Mizo speakers and learners.
- **[Mizo-godot-game](https://github.com/Kakcalu13/Mizo-godot-game)** — an educational Godot game for my children: Mizo, English, sign language, by playing.
- **[Inkhelhna-ho-te](https://github.com/Kakcalu13/Inkhelhna-ho-te)** — a small game in Mizo, named after the phrase *"let's play."*

> If you work on **low-resource language NLP, speech models, or language preservation**, I would genuinely love to talk. There is so much room for collaboration here, and the work is too important to do alone.
